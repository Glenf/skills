# NORTH-STAR.md format

The document a `north-star` session produces. One why at the root; the other four W's derive from it.

## Template

```md
# North Star — {name}

> {The star. One sentence, present tense, one idea — no conjunction welding two ideas together.}

## Why
{2-3 sentences. The bedrock need. What is actually at stake if this never happens.}

## Who
{The specific people whose need this is: named, or a group narrow enough to picture.}

## What changes
- {An observable difference once the star is true}
- {Another}

## Where it applies
{The domain this steers — and, by omission, where it does not.}

## When you would know
{The horizon, and the signal you would look for.}

## This forbids
- {A want the star says no to}
- {Another}

## Carried, not held
- {A need that belongs to someone else — employer, industry, peer group}

---
Set {date}. Revise when the why moves, not when the plan does.
```

## Rules

- **One screen.** Past that it has stopped being a compass and started being a plan. Cut until it fits.
- **The star stands alone.** Read the sentence with the scaffolding covered. If it needs the sections to make sense, it is not distilled yet.
- **Why is the root, not a column.** Every other section has to trace back to it. A `What changes` line that does not serve the why is a leftover want.
- **Concrete over abstract.** "Ship a Rust CLI my team uses daily" beats "get better at Rust". "Two founders deciding on data tooling" beats "users".
- **The user's own words** wherever they said it well. A phrase they recognise as theirs survives; your paraphrase does not.
- **`This forbids` comes from the Forbid test.** Copy what that test surfaced rather than inventing softer examples.
- **Drop `Carried, not held` when it is empty.** Keep it when it is not — it is often the most useful line on the page.
- **Date it.** A stale star steers worse than none, and the date is what makes staleness visible.
